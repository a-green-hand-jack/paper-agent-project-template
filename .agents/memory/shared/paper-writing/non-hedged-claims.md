# State the claim; keep the qualifications where they belong

## Decision

A claim is stated once, directly, at the strength the evidence supports. Hedge
words that repeat the same doubt ("might", "could potentially", "we believe
that perhaps") are removed. A real limitation, a reduced setting or a condition
of the experiment is reported where the setup is described, not sprinkled
through the claim, and never in place of the claim.

## Why

Defensive writing moves the doubt into every sentence: the reader cannot tell
which limitation is real and which is habit, and the contribution disappears
behind qualifiers. Reporting a reduced setting once, in the setup, keeps both
the claim and the limitation honest.

## Apply

- Write the claim as one sentence a reader can quote; put its conditions in the
  same paragraph as facts, not as escape clauses.
- Move "we do not claim X" statements out of the claim and into the limitations
  the venue asks for, or delete them.
- When the evidence is genuinely inconclusive, say what was measured and what
  it does not settle; that is a finding, not hedging.
- Keep the strength of an existing locked claim; weakening it is a Human
  decision.

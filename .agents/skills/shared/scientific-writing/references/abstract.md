# Abstract

The abstract is often the only part of the paper a reader sees. It must therefore carry the complete scientific message without depending on the main text.

## Use a context → content → conclusion arc

A strong abstract usually performs three jobs:

1. **Context:** establish the problem and the specific gap.
2. **Content:** state what was done and the most important results.
3. **Conclusion:** state what the results support and why they matter.

Do not begin with detailed results before the reader knows what problem they solve.

## Narrow the context quickly

Move from the broader field to the specific unresolved issue. Background should earn its place by helping the reader understand the gap.

A practical sequence is:

- broad problem,
- current limitation or unknown,
- study objective,
- approach,
- primary result(s),
- interpretation and significance.

## Make the gap and answer match

The abstract fails when its setup and result belong to different questions. Check that the result explicitly fills the gap introduced near the beginning.

Use this diagnostic pair:

> Before this study, we did not know ______.

> This study shows ______.

The second statement should resolve or materially reduce the uncertainty in the first.

## Prioritize results, not process

Method detail belongs in the abstract only when it is necessary to interpret the finding or when the method itself is the contribution. Prefer quantitative or concrete findings over procedural narration.

Avoid lists of everything measured. Select the results needed to support the central contribution.

## Keep claims calibrated

The abstract is where overclaiming is most damaging because many readers will never inspect the evidence. Avoid turning association into causation, exploratory findings into confirmatory claims, or a narrow study population into universal conclusions.

## Final abstract check

An abstract should let a reader answer:

- Why was this study needed?
- What was done?
- What was found?
- What is the main interpretation?
- What is the scope of that interpretation?

If one of these is missing, fix the argument before shortening sentences.

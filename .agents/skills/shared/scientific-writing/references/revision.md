# Revision and Manuscript Diagnosis

Revision should proceed from scientific logic to structure to sentences. Copyediting a structurally confused paper wastes effort.

## Use an outside-reader pass

Authors know too much about their own work to naturally experience the manuscript like a new reader. Review the draft as if the only available information is what is literally on the page.

Ask:

- Is the main contribution obvious?
- Does each claim have visible support?
- Are missing reasoning steps being supplied from the author's memory rather than the text?
- Are important terms defined before use?
- Does each section deliver what the previous section made the reader expect?

## Revise in layers

### Pass 1 — scientific validity

Check the research question, evidence, analysis, alternatives, and claim strength.

### Pass 2 — story and structure

Check section order, figure order, paragraph roles, repeated material, and transitions.

### Pass 3 — reader navigation

Check headings, topic sentences, definitions, references to figures, and whether dependencies are easy to follow.

### Pass 4 — prose

Only now improve concision, grammar, sentence rhythm, terminology, and formatting.

## Prefer rewriting to patching when necessary

Repeated local edits can preserve a bad structure. If a paragraph is performing several incompatible jobs, rewrite it around one purpose rather than accumulating transitional phrases.

Similarly, if the outline cannot be explained simply, return to the outline instead of polishing individual sentences.

## Seek fresh readers

Ask colleagues to explain back:

- what they think the central contribution is,
- where they became uncertain,
- which claim they found least supported,
- what they expected to see next.

A misunderstanding is evidence about the manuscript even when the author's intended meaning seems obvious.

## Pre-submission stress test

Before submission, verify:

- title, abstract, figures, and conclusion express the same core contribution,
- the introduction establishes the exact gap the results address,
- the results contain the evidence required for the main claims,
- the discussion states meaningful limitations,
- methods and analysis choices are reproducible enough for the field,
- claims do not depend on unpublished or unstated reasoning,
- obvious reviewer questions have been addressed rather than deferred.
